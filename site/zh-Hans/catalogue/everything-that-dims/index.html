Known planetsEverything that dims

A first slice, stated plainly. Sector 1: 200 of the 15,898 SPOC 2-minute targets searched (Probes only; by SetuHQ). 200 stars searched, 200 star-sectors, 936 consensus dips reviewed. This catalogue is separate from the discovery feed. Nothing here is a new or confirmed planet. A class is what the light curve's cues suggest; there is no pixel or centroid check, so a blended binary can look like anything. The planet-candidate rule found 3 on the real light curves and 2 on flux-inverted copies of the same stars (dips turned into bumps, so real dips cannot pass): an excess of +1 (95%: −3 to +5). They are not distinguishable from noise in this slice, so they are not presented as candidates. 0 match a known TOI.

Each class against its own null, measured on the same 200 star-sectors: what its rule finds where its signal cannot be. Dips: the flux turned upside down (a real dip becomes a bump; a real flare or pulsation maximum becomes a dip, so this null is not empty of real signals). Flares: downward events with the real dips masked. Variability: a red-noise copy of the star (AR(1) fitted to its bins) through the same rule, which needs the same best period (within 10%) in both halves of the light curve. Of its periodogram peaks not at a binary's period, 8 are coherent; unclassified are 20 longer than a half can test (coherence not tested), 5 at the edge of the period grid and 3 that wander between halves. Limits: it finds a 300 ppm sinusoid in strong red noise only 3/20 times (scripts/variability_rule_check.py), and cannot test periods longer than about 3.8 d in one sector. Kind is compared with kind, star-sector is the unit: the 95% interval is a paired bootstrap over star-sectors, beside McNemar's test. A class that does not pass both is not distinguishable from noise in this slice: Planet candidate, Eclipsing binary, Flare, Pulsator or rotational variable, Systematic. Of the 11 flare-class brightenings, 8 rise faster than they decay and 2 the other way (1 equal); real flares rise fast, noise has no preferred direction.

The rules, and each class against its null

First match wins, in this order: systematic, eclipsing binary, variable, then planet candidate; a brightening is a flare unless it is systematic. The rules are in src/setuhq/variables.py and are re-applied to every contributed entry on ingest.
