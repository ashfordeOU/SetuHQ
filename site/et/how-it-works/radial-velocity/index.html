Radiaalkiirus

A planet and its star both orbit their shared centre of mass, the barycentre. The star's small orbit makes its light shift back and forth: the radial-velocity curve. Its amplitude K gives the planet's minimum mass, m sin i.

The numbers

K = (2πG/P)^⅓ · Mp sin i / (M★ + Mp)^⅔ / √(1 − e²) (Gaudi & Winn 2007). The star's orbit is a★ = a · Mp/(M★ + Mp); the planet's is a · M★/(M★ + Mp). Eccentric orbits follow Kepler's equation, M = E − e sin E. Masses use the IAU nominal GM values (Prša et al. 2016).

Why Earth twins are hard

An Earth twin moves its Sun-like star by about 9 cm/s. ESPRESSO holds better than 25 cm/s within a night and about 50 cm/s over months (Pepe et al. 2021). The star's own surface (granulation, oscillations, spots) adds RV noise of order m/s (Fischer et al. 2016), larger than the signal itself.
