Chapter 10 of 15

False positives

Many transit-like dips are not planets on the target star. A dip is evidence; vetting asks what else could make it. This is where SetuHQ's own checks live.

How often, and what SetuHQ checks

QuantityValueFrom
Kepler candidates that were false positives9.4%overall; 17.7% for giants (Fressin et al. 2013)
TESS prime-mission TOIs found false by follow-up565 of 2,241 (≈25%)Guerrero et al. 2021
A 50% binary eclipse diluted 1,000 times500 ppmδ_obs = δ_true · F_target/F_total [calc]: the depth of a small planet
pi Men c's shape, T23/T140.95flat-bottomed (U), not V [calc from the transit chapter]

SetuHQ's vetting flags, from the light curve alone: odd_even_mismatch (odd and even depths differ), secondary_eclipse (a dip at phase 0.5), low_snr, too_short, edge_event and insufficient_points. It has no pixel-level centroid or difference-image check and no statistical validation, so a blended background binary can pass. See the reviewers › · See a vetted case ›

  • Guerrero et al. 2021, ApJS 254, 39 (TOI catalogue)
