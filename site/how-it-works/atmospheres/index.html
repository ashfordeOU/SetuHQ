Reading atmospheres

During a transit, a sliver of starlight passes through the planet's atmosphere at its edge. Where molecules absorb, the atmosphere is opaque higher up, so the planet looks slightly bigger at those wavelengths. SetuHQ does not do spectroscopy: this explains how JWST-class telescopes read atmospheres.

One scale height H = k_B T/(μ m_H g) of atmosphere changes the transit depth by about 2 H Rp/R★². Strong features span a few scale heights. Hot, puffy, hydrogen-rich planets around small stars give the biggest signals (Seager & Sasselov 2000; Winn 2010).

Atmosphere Lab

Switch molecules on and off and change the temperature, the mean molecular weight and how strong the bands are. The lab computes the scale height and the depth per scale height from WASP-39 b's stored mass and radii, then adds each molecule's bands. It is a toy model, not a radiative-transfer calculation: band centres are real, band widths are chosen for display, and strengths are in scale heights set by the slider.
