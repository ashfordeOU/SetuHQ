Chapter 12 of 15

Molecules and life

Each gas absorbs at its own wavelengths. A molecule's shape sets how it vibrates, and its vibrations set where it absorbs in the infrared. No single gas proves life; a biosignature needs context.

A molecule with N atoms has 3N − 6 ways to vibrate (3N − 5 if it is straight): water has three, carbon dioxide four (its bend comes twice, at right angles), methane nine. A mode absorbs infrared light only if it changes the molecule's dipole. That is why CO₂'s symmetric stretch (7.50 µm), O₂'s stretch and two of methane's four fundamentals are silent in the infrared. The bands the modes make are the ones the Reading atmospheres chapter marks: water's two stretches at 2.66 and 2.73 µm make its 2.7 µm band, methane's ν₃ the 3.3 µm band, CO₂'s asymmetric stretch the 4.26 µm band, and CO's stretch the 4.67 µm band.

Band positions: the atlas's NIST CCCBDB fundamentals, λ = 10⁴/ν̃. Displacements are symmetry-correct sketches with the centre of mass fixed and no net spin, not a force-field calculation. In every mode the atom that moves most swings 0.2 Å, and the stage states how much that exaggerates its zero-point motion, the root-mean-square displacement from ħ/2ω at the mode's frequency: for water's hydrogens in ν₁ that is 0.047 Å, so they are drawn ×4. Time is slowed 10¹³ to 10¹⁴ times, and the stage gives the factor for each mode.

H₂O Water

Geometry
bent, bond 0.958 Å, angle 104.48° (CCCBDB)
Bands (µm)
1.4, 1.9, 2.7 (fundamentals 2.66 and 2.73), 6.27
Vibrations
  • ν₁ symmetric stretch · 2.73 µm · infrared-active · drawn ×4 · the 2.7 µm band in Reading atmospheres ›
  • ν₂ bend · 6.27 µm · infrared-active · drawn ×3 · beyond the 0.5–5.1 µm window of Reading atmospheres
  • ν₃ asymmetric stretch · 2.66 µm · infrared-active · drawn ×4 · the 2.7 µm band in Reading atmospheres ›
Seen by
HST/WFC3 near 1.4 µm; JWST NIRISS, NIRSpec, MIRI
Exoplanet detections
  • HD 209458 b and XO-1 b with WFC3 (Deming et al. 2013)
  • WASP-39 b with JWST (JWST ERS Team 2023)
Why it matters
The commonest molecule in warm planet atmospheres; sets the shape of most spectra.
Without life?
Made everywhere by ordinary chemistry and outgassing. Not a biosignature.

  • ν₃ asymmetric stretch · 4.26 µm · infrared-active · drawn ×10 · the 4.26 µm band in Reading atmospheres ›

JWST NIRSpec G395H/PRISM (4.3 µm), MIRI (15 µm)

  • WASP-39 b, 26σ at 4.3 µm (JWST ERS Team 2023)
  • K2-18 b, 3σ (Madhusudhan et al. 2023)
Why it matters
Traces the planet's metal content and chemistry; a context gas for any biosignature claim.
Without life?
Volcanism and thermochemistry make it readily.

Bands (µm)
4.67 fundamental
Vibrations
  • ν stretch · 4.67 µm · infrared-active · drawn ×10 · the 4.67 µm band in Reading atmospheres ›

  • K2-18 b, 5σ (Madhusudhan et al. 2023)

With O₂ it is the classic disequilibrium pair that a biosphere could sustain.

Serpentinisation of ultramafic rock (the main abiotic source on Earth today) and thermochemistry in cool H₂ atmospheres.

Earth's most prominent biosignature gas, but only meaningful in context.
Without life?
CO₂ photolysis around some stars, or massive hydrogen escape after water loss.

Biosignature checklist

  1. Detection significance. How many sigma, from how many independent analyses?
  2. Context. The star, the planet's temperature and the other gases present (Meadows et al. 2018).
  3. Disequilibrium. Gases that would react away without a source, such as O₂ with CH₄ (Krissansen-Totton et al. 2018).
  4. Abiotic sources ruled out. Every non-living way to make the gas, checked (Schwieterman et al. 2018; Catling et al. 2018).

No gas has passed this checklist on any planet. Life has not been detected anywhere beyond Earth.

Phosphine on Venus: contested

2020: phosphine reported in Venus's clouds from JCMT and ALMA (Greaves et al. 2020). Re-analyses found no significant detection (Snellen et al. 2020; Villanueva et al. 2021), with a reply by the original team (Greaves et al. 2021). Still disputed.

  • Greaves et al. 2020, Nat. Astron. 5, 655 (phosphine on Venus)
  • Snellen et al. 2020, A&A 644, L2 (re-analysis)
  • Villanueva et al. 2021, Nat. Astron. 5, 631
  • Greaves et al. 2021, reply
  • Bains et al. 2021, Astrobiology 21, 1277

DMS on K2-18 b: tentative, debated

2023: methane and CO₂ in an H₂-rich atmosphere, with "potential signs" of DMS (Madhusudhan et al. 2023). 2025: a ~3σ MIRI feature consistent with DMS and/or DMDS (Madhusudhan et al. 2025). Independent analyses found insufficient evidence (Schmidt et al. 2025; Luque et al. 2025). Not established.

  • Madhusudhan et al. 2023, ApJL 956, L13 (K2-18 b)
  • Madhusudhan et al. 2025, ApJL 983, L40
  • Schmidt et al. 2025, re-analysis (arXiv:2501.18477)
  • Luque et al. 2025, joint analysis (arXiv:2505.13407)
