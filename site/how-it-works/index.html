How a survey works

A survey points a camera at many stars at once and measures each one's brightness again and again. Most stars never show a planet. Only planets whose orbits happen to line up with us can cross their star's face.

Why only a few transit

A planet transits only if its orbit is aligned closely enough with our line of sight. For random orientations the chance is about R★/a, the star's radius over the orbit's size (Winn 2010). Close-in planets around large stars transit more often.
