Chapter 14 of 15

Equations of life

The Drake and Seager equations are ways to organise what we do not know. They are speculative frameworks, not predictions: no number on this page is an estimate of real civilisations or of life.

N = R★ · f_p · n_e · f_l · f_i · f_c · L

Presets: the 1961 Green Bank values and the "commonly quoted set" are repeated in secondary sources; the meeting's own record was not checked. "Data where we have it" uses the measured terms below and leaves the unknown ones where you set them. R★ is stars per year; the measured Milky Way rate of 1.65 ± 0.19 M☉ per year (Licquia & Newman 2015) is a mass, not a count.

TermHow well we know itValue and source
R★Measured1.65 ± 0.19 M☉/yr star formation (Licquia & Newman 2015); stars per year needs an initial mass function
f_pMeasured≈ 1: 0.77 small planets per GK star at 50–300 d (Burke et al. 2015); ≈ 2.5 per M dwarf at P < 200 d (Dressing & Charbonneau 2015)
n_eConstrainedη⊕ = 0.37–0.60 for 0.5–1.5 R⊕ in the conservative zone around 4800–6300 K stars (Bryson et al. 2021); 11 ± 4% for 1–2 R⊕ at 1–4 × Earth's flux (Petigura et al. 2013)
f_l, f_i, f_c, LUnknownNo data. Any value is an opinion.

One number hides the uncertainty. Following Sandberg, Drexler & Ord (2018), each term is drawn from a range instead: R★ 1–100, f_p 0.1–1, n_e 0.1–1, f_i 0.001–1, f_c 0.01–1, L 100–10¹⁰ years, all log-uniform. Their f_l comes from a log-normal model of the rate at which life starts; here f_l is a log-uniform stand-in from 10⁻ᵏ to 1, with k on the slider. Draws are seeded, so the result repeats.

The Seager equation

N = N★ · F_Q · F_HZ · F_O · F_L · F_S

It counts planets where a biosignature gas could be detected, not civilisations (Seager 2018): stars observed, the fraction quiet, with rocky planets in the habitable zone, observable, with life, and whose life makes a detectable gas.

Starting values: N★ = 30,000 M dwarfs bright enough for JWST, F_Q · F_HZ = 0.15, F_O = 0.001, F_L = 1, F_S = 0.5, giving N ≈ 2. They come from secondary summaries of Seager's talk and paper, not checked against the paper here; F_L = 1 is an assumption, not a measurement. The habitable-zone fraction links to the Population chapter's figures.
