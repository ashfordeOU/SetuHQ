История и статьи

Key papers and milestones, summarised in our own words. Each links to its paper (DOI) and to the part of SetuHQ it relates to.

  1. 1992

    Wolszczan & Frail

    A planetary system around the millisecond pulsar PSR B1257+12

    Two planets found from tiny delays in a dead star's radio pulses.

    The first confirmed planets beyond the Solar System.

    Wolszczan & Frail 1992, Nature 355, 145Related ›

  2. 1995

    Mayor & Queloz

    A Jupiter-mass companion to a solar-type star

    51 Pegasi b, a giant on a 4-day orbit, found from its star's wobble.

    The first planet around a Sun-like star; Nobel Prize 2019.

    Mayor & Queloz 1995, Nature 378, 355Related ›

  3. 2000

    Charbonneau et al.; Henry et al.

    HD 209458 b seen in transit

    Two teams independently caught the same planet crossing its star.

    The first transiting planet: a radius, a density, and a way into atmospheres.

    Charbonneau et al. 2000, ApJ 529, L45 (HD 209458 b)Related ›

  4. 2000

    Seager & Sasselov

    Theoretical transmission spectra during extrasolar giant planet transits

    Predicted how starlight filtered through a planet's limb would carry sodium and potassium features.

    The blueprint for transmission spectroscopy.

    Seager & Sasselov 2000, ApJ 537, 916Related ›

  5. 2002

    Charbonneau et al.

    Detection of an extrasolar planet atmosphere

    Sodium absorption in HD 209458 b during transit, 2.3 × 10⁻⁴ deeper than the continuum.

    The first exoplanet atmosphere detected.

    Charbonneau et al. 2002, ApJ 568, 377 (sodium)Related ›

  6. 2004

    Bond et al.

    OGLE 2003-BLG-235/MOA 2003-BLG-53

    A planet with 0.4% of its star's mass revealed by a short extra brightening in a lensing event.

    The first planet found by microlensing.

    Bond et al. 2004, ApJ 606, L155Related ›

  7. 2008

    Marois et al.

    Direct imaging of multiple planets orbiting the star HR 8799

    Three giant planets photographed around a young star (a fourth followed in 2010).

    The first directly imaged multi-planet system.

    Marois et al. 2008, Science 322, 1348 (HR 8799)Related ›

  8. 2008

    Kalas et al.; Gáspár & Rieke 2020

    Fomalhaut b, then reinterpreted

    An optical image of a 'planet' later explained as an expanding dust cloud from a collision.

    A caution: an image can be dust.

    Kalas et al. 2008, Science 322, 1345 (Fomalhaut b)Related ›

  9. 2010

    Borucki et al.

    Kepler planet-detection mission: introduction and first results

    Kepler (launched 2009) stared at one field for four years.

    The start of the statistical era of exoplanets.

    Borucki et al. 2010, Science 327, 977 (Kepler)Related ›

  10. 2014

    Lissauer et al.; Rowe et al.

    Validation of Kepler's multiple-planet candidates

    Showed that candidates in multi-planet systems are almost always real, validating 851 planets in 340 systems.

    Statistical validation at scale.

    Rowe et al. 2014, ApJ 784, 45 (851 planets validated)Related ›

  11. 2015

    Ricker et al.

    Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite

    The mission design of TESS, which began observing in 2018.

    The survey SetuHQ reads.

    Ricker et al. 2015, JATIS 1, 014003 (TESS)Related ›

  12. 2019

    Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences

    Nobel Prize in Physics

    Half the prize to Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz for 51 Pegasi b.

    Recognition of the field.

    Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, Nobel Prize in Physics 2019Related ›

  13. 2023

    JWST Transiting Exoplanet Community ERS Team

    Identification of carbon dioxide in an exoplanet atmosphere

    A 26σ CO₂ feature at 4.3 µm in WASP-39 b.

    The first definitive CO₂ in an exoplanet atmosphere.

    JWST ERS Team 2023, Nature 614, 649 (CO₂ in WASP-39 b)Related ›

  14. 2026

    SetuHQ (this project)

    pi Men c recovered in a blind search of TESS sector 1

    SetuHQ's run found dips at pi Men c's period without being told where to look. pi Men c was already known (Huang et al. 2018); this is a recovery, not a discovery.

    Open the Transit case ›
