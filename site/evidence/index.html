Measured on real TOIs

The live feed's new signals are consistent with noise. Noise alone should give about 12.3 false new_candidate signals per 100 targets reviewed, against 15.8 per 100 on the live feed. Under that earlier rule a new_candidate on the feed was more likely noise than a signal.

The feed's stricter rule, pre-registered and measured again on this code. The signal must now pass in both reviewed sectors with a FAP below 0.001 over 2,000 trials. On the held-out half, noise alone gives 6.1 false signals per 100 targets (95% CI 4.0–9.4), down from 27.8 (23.1–33.1). That is still far above the 1.6 new signals per 100 targets the real light curves give, so the feed is still dominated by false alarms, and feed-level completeness fell from 39% to 17%. A feed admission is a lead for human or follow-up review, not a candidate of any confirmed status. Re-run on this code, the live feed's 19 targets admit 0 new signals.

Weights. A reviewer's weight is its calibration skill from injection tests: the share of planted transits it finds minus the share of empty light curves it marks, with a floor. The weight a run used is the one its record stores; when it differs from the calibrated weight, the run did not use the calibrated weights. Files and configuration ›

Calibration plane

Each reviewer at its false-alarm rate (x: share of empty subjects it marked) and recall (y: share of injected transits it found) in calibration. Point size is its calibrated weight. The lines join points of equal weight, weight = TPR − FPR, floored at 0.05; up and to the left is better. Stored values only.
